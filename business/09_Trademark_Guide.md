## Why this matters

A registered trademark makes **"Hagiazo"** legally yours for the things you do. It lets you
stop copycats, it's an asset the whole house is built on, and it's **far cheaper to secure
now** — before Hagiazo is well known — than to fight over later. *"Hagiazo"* is a strong mark:
it's **distinctive** (an unusual word, not a description of hair), which trademarks protect well.

## What to register — the word first

- **Register the WORD MARK "HAGIAZO"** (plain text). This protects the *name* however it's
  written — any font, any logo. It's the strongest, most flexible protection for a house of
  ventures. **[BEST PRACTICE — do this first.]**
- You *can* also register the **logo/monogram** separately, later, once the parent mark is
  designed. The word mark matters most.
- Use **™** next to Hagiazo from today (no registration needed); switch to **®** only once
  it's registered (using ® before then is an offence).

## The classes — what you're protecting it *for*

A trademark only protects the **classes** (categories of goods/services) you register. There
are 45. You **can't add classes to a granted mark later** — you'd file a fresh application —
so choose with the house in mind. *(Confirm exact classes at gov.uk; a wrong class is the
commonest DIY mistake.)*

| Class | Covers | For which venture | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| **44** | Hairdressing, beauty & wellness salon services | **Hair**, Beauty, Wellness | **Now (core)** |
| **26** | Wigs, false hair, hair pieces & accessories (goods) | Hair (wigs) | Now, if you sell wig units |
| **3** | Cosmetics, hair & beauty products (goods) | Beauty, Homeware | If/when you sell products |
| **35** | Retail / online-shop services | Any venture selling goods | If/when you sell online |
| **41** | Classes, workshops, training | Any (teaching) | If you teach |
| 20 / 21 / 24 | Furniture / homeware / textiles | **Homes**, Homeware | When Homes gets real |

> *In plain English: a trademark is fenced off by category. "Hagiazo" in the hair-and-beauty
> category (44) is the must-have now. Add the categories for wigs, products or homeware only
> when you actually sell in them — paying for categories you won't use for years just ties up
> money, and you can always file again.*

**Lean recommendation:** file the **word mark "HAGIAZO" in Class 44 now** (your live venture),
plus **26 and/or 35** only if you're already selling wigs/products. Revisit **Homes/Homeware
classes (20/21/24)** when that venture approaches.

## What it costs *(verify current fees — these change)*

- **Standard online application:** about **£170** for the first class, **~£50** for each
  additional class. **[ASSUMPTION — verify at gov.uk.]**
- **"Right Start"** option lets you pay in two halves (part now, the rest once the IPO says
  it's likely to be accepted) — useful if you want to test the water.
- Doing it yourself online is fine for one core class; a **trademark attorney** (their fee on
  top) is worth it for getting the house's classes right.

## The steps

1. **Search first (free).** Use *Search for a trade mark* on gov.uk to check nobody already
   has "Hagiazo" (or something confusingly similar) in your classes. Also check Companies
   House, domains and social handles.
2. **Pick your classes** (see above).
3. **Apply online** at gov.uk — the word "HAGIAZO", your classes, your details, pay the fee.
4. **Examination (~2–3 weeks).** The IPO checks it and sends a report; respond if they raise anything.
5. **Publication & opposition (2 months).** It's published in the Trade Marks Journal; anyone
   who objects has two months to say so. Most marks sail through.
6. **Registration (~4 months total).** You get your certificate. The mark lasts **10 years**
   and is renewable indefinitely. Keep **using** it — unused marks can be challenged.
