## 1. Tax — HMRC  **[REGULATORY]**

**Register as self-employed for Self Assessment with HMRC.**
*Citation: Income Tax (Trading and Other Income) Act 2005; HMRC Self Assessment rules.*
- **Deadline:** register by **5 October** after the end of the tax year (6 Apr–5 Apr)
  in which you started trading. *(This date is stable year to year.)*
- You pay **Income Tax** and **National Insurance** on your profits via a yearly tax return.
- **Class 4 NICs** apply to profits above the Lower Profits Limit; since **2024/25**,
  **Class 2 NICs** are no longer physically due for most self-employed above the Small
  Profits Threshold (you're treated as having paid them, keeping your State Pension record).
- **[ASSUMPTION — verify]** Exact thresholds and the **£1,000 trading allowance** and
  **£90,000 VAT threshold** — confirm current-year figures at gov.uk.

> *In plain English: tell HMRC you're working for yourself, keep a record of what you
> earn and spend, and once a year fill in a tax return and pay the tax owed. Register
> by 5 October after your first tax year so you're not late.*

**Actions:** ☐ Register at gov.uk/set-up-self-employed ☐ Keep every receipt (expenses
lower your tax) ☐ Set aside ~20–30% of profit for tax ☐ Diarise 31 Jan filing deadline.

## 2. Insurance  **[BEST PRACTICE — effectively essential]**

- **Public liability** — covers a client/member of the public being injured or their
  property damaged. *Not required by statute for a solo trader, but effectively
  essential, and often required by any venue or chair-rental you use.*
- **Treatment / professional liability** — covers a claim arising from the service
  itself (a reaction, damage to hair).
- **Product liability** — covers products you apply or sell.
- **Employers' Liability — [REGULATORY] *if you employ anyone*.**
  *Citation: Employers' Liability (Compulsory Insurance) Act 1969 — minimum £5m cover.*
  Not needed while you are genuinely solo.

> *In plain English: if a client trips, reacts badly, or blames you for damage, insurance
> pays the claim instead of you. You don't legally have to hold it as a one-person
> business — but going without it risks everything you own. The moment you hire even one
> person, employers' liability insurance becomes the law.*

**Actions:** ☐ Get quotes (Salon Gold, Simply Business, Policybee, Insync) ☐ Confirm it
covers **braids, locs, and every service you actually offer** ☐ Keep the certificate here.

## 3. Data protection — UK GDPR & the ICO  **[REGULATORY]**

**You process personal data** (client names, phone numbers, booking history, photos),
so data-protection law applies.
*Citation: UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018.*
- Most organisations that process personal data must **pay the ICO data protection
  fee** unless they qualify for an exemption. For a small business this is typically
  the **Tier 1 fee (£40, or £35 by direct debit)** — **[ASSUMPTION — verify current fee]**.
- Some very limited processing is exempt — **take the ICO self-assessment** to be sure.
  Storing client contact details and doing marketing usually means you **do** need to register.

> *In plain English: because you keep clients' contact details and photos, the law says
> you must look after that information and, in most cases, pay a small yearly fee (around
> £40) to the ICO and be on their register. Do their 2-minute checker to confirm.*

**Actions:** ☐ Take the self-assessment at ico.org.uk ☐ Register + pay the fee if
required ☐ Follow the privacy policy in **BIZ-01** ☐ Get **photo consent** before posting.

## 4. Consumer protection  **[REGULATORY]**

**Services must be performed with reasonable care and skill**, within a reasonable time,
for a reasonable price where not agreed in advance.
*Citation: Consumer Rights Act 2015.*

> *In plain English: do the job properly and as described. If something genuinely goes
> wrong through your work, the client has a right to it being put right. Clear prices and
> a written booking/cancellation policy (BIZ-04) prevent most disputes.*

**Actions:** ☐ Publish clear prices ☐ Publish a booking/cancellation policy ☐ Keep a
simple complaints route (see BIZ-01 contact).

## 5. Health & safety  **[REGULATORY where your work risks others]**

*Citation: Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974; COSHH Regulations 2002 for any chemical products.*
- As self-employed, if your work can affect the health & safety of clients or others,
  the general duties apply. A **written basic risk assessment** is expected once you deal
  with the public.
- **If you use any chemical products** (colour, relaxer, adhesives), **COSHH** applies and
  a **patch test** protocol becomes important (see BIZ-05).

> *In plain English: keep your space clean and safe, sanitise tools, and think through
> what could hurt a client and how you prevent it — written down. If you ever use hair
> chemicals, there are extra rules and you must patch-test first.*

**Actions:** ☐ Write a one-page risk assessment ☐ Tool hygiene/sanitation routine ☐ First
aid kit ☐ Confirm whether you offer any chemical service (changes the rules).

## 6. Local licensing — Harlow District Council  **[REGULATORY — verify locally]**

- **Braiding, cornrows, twists and locs are normally NOT licensable.** Special-treatments
  / skin-piercing licences (under local government provisions) generally target
  piercing, tattooing, electrolysis and semi-permanent make-up — **not braiding**.
- **This must be confirmed directly with Harlow District Council**, because local rules
  vary and any move into piercing/chemical/scalp treatments can change the answer.

> *In plain English: you almost certainly don't need a council licence just to braid — but
> ring Harlow Council once to confirm for your exact services, and note the date you checked.*

**Actions:** ☐ Call/email Harlow District Council environmental health ☐ Record the answer
+ date in the status table above ☐ Re-check before adding any new treatment type.
